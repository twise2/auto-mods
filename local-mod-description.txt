Exploding Kings: Kings explode on death in a chain reaction, and can garrison siege weapons or be converted like any other unit. Rewarding Snipes: when a King dies it leaves behind a permanent, always-visible relic cart (shown as an emperor's litter) worth 50 bonus population, so killing an enemy King is a real, lasting reward for whoever secures the kill. Heroes & Villains: every civilization gets a named historical hero - a land hero trainable at the Castle and, for civs with a strong naval history, a water hero trainable at the Dock - including the new Saxons (Tostig Godwinson), Varangians (Harald, plus the longship Dreki), and Danes (Rollo). Heroes cost 500 food and 500 gold and are limited to one at a time. Each hero grants a passive aura based on its type, borrowed from existing hero abilities: Cavalry and Warships make nearby military units move faster, Cavalry Archers and Conquistadors make them attack faster, Infantry give nearby units +2 armor and extra attack (Battle Horn), Archers and Hand Cannoneers give nearby ranged units +1 range (Borrowed Arrows), and Monks heal nearby units. Units inside a hero's aura glow. Civilization Identity Expansion: dozens of regional and historical grants giving civs more of what they'd plausibly have fielded, reusing only units and buildings that already exist in the game. Steppe Lancer, Elephant Archer, Armored Elephant, and Genitour extended to civs with matching cavalry heritage. Varangian Guard extended to Slavs, whose Kievan Rus' ancestors sent the first Varangians to Byzantium. Camel Scout given as an early option to camel-heritage civs, with Berbers, Saracens, Hindustanis, and Ethiopians starting the game with a Camel Scout instead of a Scout Cavalry. Winged Hussars, Legionary, Condottiero, Thirisadai, Slingers, and War Chariots extended to fitting civs. Missionaries and Warrior Priests extended to more civs with a matching religious tradition. Settlement extended to Aztecs, Mayans, and Incas, replacing their Mill, Lumber Camp, and Mining Camp from the start; Folwark replaces the Mill for Bohemians and Lithuanians. Mule Carts extended to more nomadic civs. Fire Lancer, Rocket Cart, Traction Trebuchet, and Lou Chuan extended across East Asian civs. Hei-Kuang Cavalry given to Chinese as a Knight-line replacement; Grenadier given to Chinese, Khitans, Vietnamese, Mongols, Koreans, and Turks in place of Hand Cannoneer. Jian Swordsman extended to Shu and Wei; Temple Guard extended to Incas and Aztecs. Knight/Cavalier/Paladin removed, both the units and the research, for civs whose real identity replaces it - Turks, Huns, Berbers, Saracens, Malay, Burmese, Khmer, Vietnamese, and Chinese. Regional buildings extended: Caravanserai, Feitoria, Folwark, Donjon, Krepost, and Fortified Church. Regional skins (cosmetic only, stats unchanged): civs in the same region share a historically themed look for their Champion, Pikeman, Knight, Cavalier, Paladin, Hussar, Heavy Cavalry Archer, Elite Steppe Lancer, and Elite Genitour, drawn from existing hero models - for example Bohemond and Kestutis for Norse and Eastern European Knights and Cavaliers, Wang Tong for Central and East Asian Cavaliers, Frankish Paladin for the French, and Crusader Knight for the Teutons. Samurai gained a situational ranged-attack swap; Longboats can now transport units. Community Games balance changes: all towers - Watch Tower, Guard Tower, Keep, and Bombard Tower - now cost 1 population each. Trade Caravan research is significantly more expensive (800 food, 200 gold).
